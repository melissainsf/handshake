Build the employer journey from the bottom up

Design the fully automated journey for the lowest-ARR employers first. Then carry it up every tier, adding human touches, or swapping them in where testing shows a person wins. Gainsight and Appcues stay as the delivery layer; AI sits in the decision layer between them.

How to read this page. Everything about Handshake's internals (data, metrics, tooling setup) is an assumption from public materials and the brief, marked to validate. Every number in the prototype is fictional.

The thesis

Most journeys are designed top-down: the white-glove enterprise motion, then stripped down for the long tail. I'd do the reverse.

Base

One automated journey

Built for the long tail first: every milestone has an automated touch (Gainsight Journey Orchestrator email, Appcues in-app) and a self-serve path.

Added

Human touches layered on

Mid-Market and Enterprise get the same base, plus human touches where a person's time is worth it.

Swapped

Or swapped, when tested

Where a test shows a human touch beats the automated one for a tier, the outreach is swapped. The self-serve tool behind it never goes away.

Why this way round

Every customer can self-serve. A Tier 1 recruiter who wants to fix a posting at 11pm can, because the base exists for everyone.
Tiers become explicit decisions. Each human touch is a deliberate "this is worth a person" call, testable against the base, not a default nobody questions.

How swaps are decided: split a tier's accounts, half get the automated touch, half the human one; compare on the milestone the touch is meant to move; keep the winner.

Question 1

First program: Milestone 2 on autopilot, paced by the season

One digital engagement program to prioritize first: what it is, what problem it solves, and why it's first.

What it is

An automated program that gets every new long-tail employer from signed to a live posting that is drawing quality applicants (Handshake's Milestone 2) before their current recruiting window closes. Delivered through Gainsight JO emails and Appcues guides on the posting pages; paced by how many weeks are left in the window.

The problem it solves

A low-ARR employer whose first posting quietly underperforms has no CSM to notice. They conclude "Handshake doesn't work for us", and we find out at renewal, a season too late. Today's lifecycle emails are calendar-based, so they can't tell a thriving posting from a dead one.

Why it's first

1) Quality applicants is the value an employer pays for, so it's the milestone closest to revenue. 2) Everything after it (promotions, measuring, renewal, expansion) depends on it. 3) It's the long tail, where bottom-up starts. 4) The season is a hard clock: a week lost in October can't be bought back in March. 5) It runs on data that already exists: postings and applicants to validate.

Question 2

AI workflow: the underperforming-posting rescue

Specified at step level. It serves Milestone 2 for every tier; only where the human sits changes.

1

Trigger, and what fires it

Condition: a posting has been live 7 days (5 if under 4 weeks remain in the window) with applicants below 50% of the benchmark for comparable postings: same role family, location type, and school targeting.

What fires it: a nightly job over product data computes each live posting's applicants against its benchmark and writes a signal to Gainsight, which opens the workflow. Suppressed if: the posting fired in the last 14 days, the posting is closed, or the account already has an open CSM task.

2

Gather inputs, and where each comes from

InputSourceWhy it's needed
Posting fields: title, pay, location, schools targeted, description, template usedHandshake product dataWhat we're diagnosing
Views → applies funnel for this postingProduct analyticsTells a visibility problem from a conversion problem
Benchmark: comparable postings that hit target, and what they had in commonData warehouseGrounds every suggestion in evidence
Tier, ARR, renewal date, health score, CSM, prior touchesGainsightRouting and tone; no repeat of an ignored suggestion
In-app guide history: seen, dismissed, completedAppcuesDon't re-show what they dismissed
Call summaries, if any (usually none in the long tail)GongContext: hiring plans, stated goals
Posting best-practice playbooks and help articlesGleanThe fix links to Handshake's own guidance
Season calendar: days left in the current windowConfigUrgency and pacing
3

Diagnose system decides

An LLM (Claude or ChatGPT, whichever wins our evaluation set) compares the posting with the benchmark and returns a structured result: up to three likely causes, each tied to a specific field and the benchmark evidence, plus a confidence score. Numbers are passed in from the data, never generated.

4

Draft the fix system decides

The specific edits (e.g. "add a pay range", "add these 6 schools that fill similar roles"), an email from the account's owner (the scaled CS team in the long tail, the named CSM above it), and an Appcues guide on the posting's edit page that walks through the same edits with one click to apply.

5

Automated checks system decides

Every number in the draft matches an input; no promises of outcomes; links resolve; tone and brand rules pass; confidence is above threshold. Any failure routes the draft to a person instead of sending.

6

Route by tier the human line

TierAt launchWhy the line is there
Long tailA person approves every draft (Phase 1), then a sample (Phase 2+)Until we've measured accuracy, one wrong diagnosis at scale is thousands of wrong emails
Mid-MarketThe account's CSM approves or edits in a Gainsight task; 1-business-day deadline, then it falls back to the base emailA revenue-bearing relationship: the CSM owns what the customer hears
EnterpriseThe CSM delivers the fix personally; AI output is a prep briefHighest ARR, multi-team accounts: the diagnosis is input to a conversation, not a message
Any tier, renewal within 90 daysAlways a personA wrong message during renewal costs more than a slow one
7

Send, then learn system

Sent via Gainsight JO and Appcues. Seven days later the workflow records whether the fix was applied and what applicants did, and adds the case to the evaluation set that the diagnosis step is re-tested against.

What I'd let it do on its own once trusted, and what has to be true first

Phase 0 · shadow

AI drafts, people send their own

Compare AI diagnoses with what CSMs would have said. Builds the evaluation set.

Phase 1

A person approves every draft

Entry: AI matches the CSM diagnosis on 85%+ of 200 shadow cases.

Phase 2

Long tail sends on its own

Entry: 95%+ approved with light or no edits over 4 weeks; zero factual errors in audit; lift over holdout. A 10% weekly sample is still audited.

Phase 3

Pre-fills the fix in the posting

The customer still clicks "apply"; we never edit a posting without consent. Entry: Phase 2 stable for a full season; legal and brand sign-off.

Mid-Market and Enterprise stay human-in-the-loop by design (the tier choice), not because the AI isn't trusted.

Question 3

How we'd know either one worked

Leading and lagging indicators, what we compare against, and the number that shuts it off, for the program and the workflow separately.

The program (Question 1)

Leading: % of new employers reaching Milestone 2 before their window closes · median days signed → M2 · M1 checklist completion.

Lagging: first-season results (applicants per posting, roles filled) · logo renewal and GRR for the long-tail cohort · NRR including expansion into promotions, AI Sourcing and Insights Pro to validate.

Compared against: a randomized 15% holdout of new long-tail employers who get today's lifecycle emails, in the same season, so seasonal swings can't fake a win.

Shut it off if: after one full window, M2-before-close is not at least 5 points above holdout; or unsubscribes exceed 0.5% per send, or spam complaints 0.1%.

The AI workflow (Question 2)

Leading: draft approval rate and how much people edit · fix applied within 7 days · applicants in the 7 days after, against flagged postings that got nothing · CSM minutes per rescue.

Lagging: postings reaching benchmark · roles filled · renewal of accounts that were rescued.

Compared against: 20% of flagged postings randomly held out (no rescue), measured over the same weeks.

Shut it off if: any factual error goes out without approval (auto-send pauses immediately) · approval rate under 80% for 2 weeks (back to Phase 1) · no lift over holdout after 4 weeks (off).

Team efficiency, from the job description: accounts per CSM, CSM hours per account, and the self-serve rate in upper tiers (issues solved through the base without the CSM). All thresholds are starting points to calibrate, not facts about Handshake.
