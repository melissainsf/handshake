Build the employer journey from the bottom up

Design the fully automated journey for the lowest-ARR employers first. Then carry it up to Mid-Market, adding human touches, or swapping them in where testing shows a person wins. Enterprise accounts belong to the Enterprise team and are out of scope. Gainsight and Appcues stay as the delivery layer; AI sits in the decision layer between them.

Gainsight and Appcues remain the delivery layer: every email and guide on this page goes out through them. Nothing is replaced.

Question 1

First program: Milestone 2 on autopilot, paced by the season

From the brief1. One digital engagement program you would prioritize first. What it is, what problem it solves, and why it's first.
What it is

An automated program that gets every new SMB employer from signed to a live posting that is drawing quality applicants (Handshake's Milestone 2) before their current recruiting window closes. Delivered through Gainsight JO emails and Appcues guides on the posting pages; paced by how many weeks are left in the window.

What problem it solves

A low-ARR employer whose first posting quietly underperforms has no CSM to notice. They conclude "Handshake doesn't work for us", and we find out at renewal, a season too late. Lifecycle emails that run on a schedule, rather than on posting results, can't tell a thriving posting from a dead one to validate: how today's programs trigger.

Why it's first

1) Quality applicants is the value an employer pays for, so it's the milestone closest to revenue. 2) Everything after it (promotions, measuring, renewal, expansion) depends on it. 3) It's SMB, where bottom-up starts. 4) The season is a hard clock: a week lost in October can't be bought back in March. 5) It runs on data that already exists: postings and applicants to validate.

Question 2

AI workflow: the underperforming-posting rescue

From the brief2. One AI-enabled workflow, specified at step level. A box labeled "AI" is not a design. We need: the trigger, and what fires it · the inputs, and where each one comes from · what the system decides on its own, and what a human approves, and why the line is there · what you'd let it do autonomously once you trusted it, and what would have to be true first.

Seven steps. It serves Milestone 2 for SMB and Mid-Market; Enterprise accounts are excluded. Each of the brief's four asks is labelled where it's answered.

1

The trigger, and what fires it Brief ask 1 of 4

Condition: a posting has been live 7 days (5 if under 4 weeks remain in the window) with applicants below 50% of the benchmark for comparable postings: same role family, location type, and school targeting.

What fires it: a nightly job over product data computes each live posting's applicants against its benchmark and writes a signal to Gainsight, which opens the workflow. Excluded: Enterprise accounts (owned by the Enterprise team). Suppressed if: the posting fired in the last 14 days, the posting is closed, or the account already has an open CSM task.

3

Diagnose system decides

An LLM (Claude or ChatGPT, whichever wins our evaluation set) compares the posting with the benchmark and returns a structured result: up to three likely causes, each tied to a specific field and the benchmark evidence, plus a confidence score. Numbers are passed in from the data, never generated.

4

Draft the fix system decides

The specific edits (e.g. "add a pay range", "add these 6 schools that fill similar roles"), an email from the account's owner (the scaled CS team in SMB, the named CSM above it), and an Appcues guide on the posting's edit page that walks through the same edits with one click to apply.

5

Automated checks system decides

Every number in the draft matches an input; no promises of outcomes; links resolve; tone and brand rules pass; confidence is above threshold. Any failure routes the draft to a person instead of sending.

Sent via Gainsight JO and Appcues. Seven days later the workflow records whether the fix was applied and what applicants did, and adds the case to the evaluation set that the diagnosis step is re-tested against.

4 · Guardrails

Read-only by default. Connectors start with read access. Anything that writes (sends a message, changes a CRM field, publishes a guide) goes through a person until it has earned autonomy, the same ladder as the posting rescue.

Beyond the three questions

The bigger picture: where this program fits

The thinking behind the answers: how I'd design the whole employer journey, and where the first program sits in it.

The thesis

Most journeys are designed top-down: the white-glove motion first, then stripped down for SMB. I'd do the reverse, across the two tiers this role owns: SMB and Mid-Market.

Base

One automated journey

Built for SMB first: every milestone has an automated touch (Gainsight Journey Orchestrator email, Appcues in-app) and a self-serve path.

Added

Human touches layered on

Mid-Market gets the same base, plus human touches where a CSM's time is worth it.

Swapped

Or swapped, when tested

Where a test shows a human touch beats the automated one for Mid-Market, the outreach is swapped. The self-serve tool behind it never goes away.

Why this way round

Every customer can self-serve. A Mid-Market recruiter who wants to fix a posting at 11pm can, without waiting for their CSM, because the base exists for everyone.
Human touches become explicit decisions. Each one is a deliberate "this is worth a CSM's time" call, testable against the base, not a default nobody questions.

How swaps are decided: split Mid-Market accounts, half get the automated touch, half the human one; compare on the milestone the touch is meant to move; keep the winner.
